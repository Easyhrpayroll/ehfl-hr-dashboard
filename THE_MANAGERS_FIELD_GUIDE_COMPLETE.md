## KEY FRAMEWORKS

### 1. The Six Core Leadership Responsibilities (Chapter 3)
1. **Create Direction** — People know where they're going
2. **Take Responsibility** — For people, not just results
3. **Build Capability** — Through work, coaching, stretch
4. **Create Accountability** — Clear expectations + follow-up
5. **Build Trust** — Through honesty and consistency
6. **Balance Compassion and Courage** — Care + truth

### 2. Results + Relationships Framework (Chapter 7)
- **Outcomes Clarity:** Specific, measurable, understood
- **Autonomy + Boundaries:** Real decision authority within limits
- **Support & Coaching:** Available, helpful, developmental
- **Accountability:** Clear expectations, addressed promptly
- **Recognition:** Notice wins, growth, and effort

### 3. The ASK Framework for Job Design (Chapter 9)
- **A = Accountability:** Specific outcomes (what they're responsible for)
- **S = Skills:** Behavioral capabilities required
- **K = Knowledge:** Information they need to know

### 4. Capability × Commitment Matrix (Chapter 12)
| | High Commitment | Low Commitment |
|---|---|---|
| **High Capability** | A Players | Mercenaries |
| **Low Capability** | Learners | Problem Employees |

### 5. The Feedback Framework (Chapter 16)
1. Set up well (private, intentional)
2. Describe behavior (specific, observable)
3. Explain impact (why it matters)
4. Ask perspective (listen)
5. Partner on solution (collaborative)
6. Follow up (check progress)

## CONVERSATION SCRIPTS YOU CAN USE

**Setting expectations:** "Here's our goal: 500 loans this quarter. Here's why it matters: [business reason]. Here's what success looks like: [specific outcome]. Here's how I'll support you: [specific support]."

**Giving feedback:** "I want to give you feedback. In the call yesterday, you interrupted the customer. I noticed you did this four times. When that happens, customers feel rushed. Here's what might work better: [specific approach]."

**Difficult conversation:** "I want to be direct. Your performance isn't meeting expectations. Here's what I'm observing: [specific examples]. This needs to change. I want to understand what's happening. Help me understand."

**Recognition:** "I saw how you handled that customer problem. You listened fully, asked clarifying questions, then proposed a solution. That's exactly what I want. It builds loyalty."

**Delegating:** "I want to develop you. I'm going to teach you this process. First we'll do it together. Then you'll lead and I'll support. By month three, you'll own it."

## KEY PRINCIPLES

### 1. **Your Team Is a Mirror**
If your team lacks accountability, you probably do too. If they avoid difficult conversations, you probably do. Change yourself first.

### 2. **Leadership Is Learnable**
You don't need to be perfect at this. You need to practice, get feedback, and improve. Every manager in this guide learned over time.

### 3. **People Develop Through Work**
Training helps. But real development happens when you give people real work, they struggle, they learn, and they grow.

### 4. **Both Results AND Relationships Matter**
Results without relationships = burnout and turnover. Relationships without results = mediocrity. You need both.

### 5. **Feedback Is a Gift**
When you give feedback, you're investing in someone's growth. Frame it that way. People respond.

### 6. **Different People Need Different Things**
Your A Player needs autonomy. Your Learner needs structure. Your Mercenary needs clarity. Don't manage everyone the same.

### 7. **Small Habits Create Culture**
You don't transform culture through big speeches. You transform it through small, consistent actions. Recognition. Feedback. Follow-through.

## AUTHOR'S NOTE

This guide comes from three sources of wisdom:

**Simon Sinek** taught us that "Leadership is not about being in charge. It's about taking care of those in your charge." This is the heart of leadership.

**Jim Rohn** reminded us that "Income does not exceed personal development." Your team's ceiling is your ceiling as a person. Develop yourself first.

**Rajiv Talreja** showed us that different people need different management. The Capability × Commitment Matrix is one of the most powerful tools for understanding your team.

These principles, combined with real scenarios from Indian home finance, create a practical guide for managers who want to build high-performing teams while developing people.
