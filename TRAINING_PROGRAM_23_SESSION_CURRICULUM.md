# THE MANAGER'S FIELD GUIDE
## 23-Session Training Program Curriculum

**Duration:** 6 months | 1 session per week | 90 minutes per session  
**Format:** In-person or virtual | Small group preferred (8-15 people)  
**Materials:** The Manager's Field Guide + Appendices + Slide deck (suggested)

---

## SESSION STRUCTURE (Same for all 23 sessions)

**Opening (10 min):** 
- What we're covering
- Why it matters
- How it connects to your role

**Content (30 min):**
- Concept explanation
- Real scenarios from home finance
- Key frameworks

**Exercise (25 min):**
- Group activity or individual reflection
- Use appendix template
- Pair share

**Discussion (20 min):**
- Questions
- How does this apply to you?
- Challenges and solutions

**Action (5 min):**
- This week's action
- Accountability partnership
- Preview next session

## PART 1: START WITH YOURSELF (Weeks 1-4)

### SESSION 1: Before You Lead Others, Assess Yourself

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand how team behavior reflects leadership
- Complete honest self-assessment
- Identify leadership gaps

**Key Concept:** Your team is a mirror. Before you fix them, look in the mirror.

**Exercise:**
- Complete Appendix 1 (Self-Assessment Scorecard)
- Identify top 3 areas to develop
- Share one result with accountability partner

**Discussion Questions:**
1. What surprised you about your score?
2. What area feels most important to work on?
3. How might your team see you differently than you see yourself?

**This Week's Action:**
- Ask one person (peer/boss/mentor): "What do you notice about my leadership?"

---

### SESSION 2: Manager vs. Leader — The Transition

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand the fundamental shift from doing to developing
- Recognize when you're still operating as individual contributor
- Commit to the transition

**Key Concept:** Your old job (doing the work) is not your new job (developing people).

**Real Scenario:**
- Rahul: Best salesman promoted to sales manager
- Problem: Still taking all the big accounts
- Solution: Hands them off, coaches team
- Result: Team capability increased in 6 months

**Exercise:**
- Identify 3 tasks you're doing that someone could learn
- For each: Write who should do it, what they'd learn, how you'd coach
- Share plan with group

**Discussion Questions:**
1. What's hardest about letting go of technical work?
2. When have you done this successfully before?
3. What would change if you stopped doing the technical work?

**This Week's Action:**
- Hand off one task to someone on your team
- Tell them: "I'm doing this to develop you. Here's how I'll support you."

---

### SESSION 3: What Successful Leaders Actually Do

**Learning Objectives:**
- Learn six core leadership responsibilities
- See how they connect
- Start applying them

**Key Concept:** Leadership is not a position. It's a set of behaviors.

**Six Responsibilities:**
1. Create Direction (people know where they're going)
2. Take Responsibility (for people, not just results)
3. Build Capability (through work, coaching, stretch)
4. Create Accountability (clear expectations + follow-up)
5. Build Trust (through honesty and consistency)
6. Balance Compassion & Courage (care + truth)

**Exercise:**
- Observe one meeting you run this week
- Score yourself on all six (1-5 scale)
- Bring results to next session

**Discussion Questions:**
1. Which of the six do you do well?
2. Which is hardest for you?
3. What would change if you focused on all six equally?

**This Week's Action:**
- In one conversation, do all six: Set direction, take responsibility, build capability, create accountability, build trust, show both compassion and courage.

---

### SESSION 4: Leadership as a Skill (Not a Talent)

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand that leadership is learnable
- Create personal development plan
- Commit to deliberate practice

**Key Concept:** You don't need to be perfect. You need to practice and improve.

**Deliberate Practice Framework:**
1. Identify what's hard
2. Break it into pieces
3. Practice the hard part
4. Get feedback
5. Adjust technique
6. Practice again

**Exercise:**
- Identify one leadership skill you're weak in
- Create 90-day development plan
- Identify who will help you (mentor/coach/peer)
- Schedule first feedback conversation

**Discussion Questions:**
1. What skill are you committing to develop?
2. Why this skill? What will change when you improve?
3. Who will help you stay accountable?

**This Week's Action:**
- Schedule one feedback conversation with your development mentor
- Ask: "How am I doing as a leader? What should I focus on?"

---

## PART 2: UNDERSTANDING YOURSELF AS A PERSON (Weeks 5-6)

### SESSION 5: Personal Development — The Foundation

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand personal development as leadership foundation
- Assess your own development
- Create plan

**Key Concept:** Income does not exceed personal development. Your team's ceiling is your ceiling.

**Five Levels of Development:**
1. Self-awareness (what you don't see)
2. Skill development (intentional growth)
3. Value alignment (living what you believe)
4. Character building (who you are when no one's watching)
5. Purpose clarity (why this work matters)

**Exercise:**
- Self-awareness: Ask someone you trust—What do you see about me that I might not?
- Skill: Pick one skill to build and commit to practicing
- Values: Write top 3 values and assess alignment
- Character: Identify one character-building action
- Purpose: Write your leadership purpose in one sentence

**Discussion Questions:**
1. What area of personal development resonates most?
2. Where's the biggest gap between who you are and who you want to be?
3. How is your personal development (or lack of it) showing up in your leadership?

**This Week's Action:**
- Do one concrete personal development action: Read, practice a skill, admit a mistake, help without getting credit.

---

### SESSION 6: Your Team Reflects Your Leadership

**Learning Objectives:**
- See connection between leadership and team behavior
- Identify patterns
- Create change plan

**Key Concept:** When you see a problem in your team, look for it in yourself first.

**Culture Creation Formula:**
- Your values → Your decisions → Your actions → Your habits → Team culture

**Exercise:**
- Identify one team pattern that frustrates you
- Ask: Do I have this pattern too? In what form?
- How can I model something different?
- What one behavior will I change this week?

**Discussion Questions:**
1. What team pattern frustrates you most?
2. How does your leadership contribute to it?
3. If you changed, what would change about the team?

**This Week's Action:**
- Change one behavior of yours that you think contributes to a team pattern
- Notice what happens

## PART 3: BUILDING CULTURE (Week 7)

### SESSION 7: Results + Relationships — The Culture That Works

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand Results + Relationships framework
- Assess current culture
- Create culture improvement plan

**Key Concept:** Both matter equally. Results without relationships = burnout. Relationships without results = mediocrity.

**Five-Step Framework:**
1. Clarity on Outcomes (what success looks like)
2. Authority to Act (autonomy + boundaries)
3. Real Support (coaching, not directing)
4. Accountability (clear, constructive)
5. Recognition (notice wins and growth)

**Exercise:**
- Use Appendix 4 (Culture Assessment)
- Score your team on all five areas
- Identify weakest area
- Create 30-day improvement plan for that area

**Scenario Work:**
- Watch video: Manager who transformed culture through Results + Relationships
- Discuss: What changed? Why did it work?
- Apply to your team: What's your first action?

**Discussion Questions:**
1. Which element of Results + Relationships is strongest in your team?
2. Which is weakest?
3. What's one thing you'll change in your team this month?

**This Week's Action:**
- Pick one element (Outcomes, Autonomy, Support, Accountability, Recognition)
- Do one concrete action to strengthen it
- Report back next week

## PART 4: HIRING (Weeks 8-11)

### SESSION 8: Why Hiring Is Your Most Important Decision

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand hiring ROI
- Know costs of bad hiring
- Commit to rigorous process

**Key Concept:** Bad hire costs $500K. Good hiring investment is $9K. It's the most profitable work you'll do.

**The Math:**
- Bad hire: Salary, benefits, training wasted + team productivity hit + recruiting to replace
- Good hiring: Slightly longer search + better fit = years of good performance

**Exercise:**
- Analyze a hiring decision you made
- Score: Capability fit? Cultural fit? Long-term success?
- What would you do differently?
- Learn from it

**Discussion Questions:**
1. What's the cost of a bad hire in your experience?
2. What's the ROI of a good hire?
3. Are you spending enough time on hiring?

**This Week's Action:**
- If hiring: Commit to meeting all 5 candidates before deciding
- If not hiring: Think through: Who would I replace first? What went wrong with them?

---

### SESSION 9: Job Design — The ASK Framework

**Learning Objectives:**
- Learn ASK framework
- Design job clearly
- Screen candidates against requirements

**Key Concept:** A = Accountability (outcomes), S = Skills (capabilities), K = Knowledge (info needed)

**Exercise:**
- If hiring: Use Appendix 5 to design job using ASK
- If not hiring: Use ASK to assess current team member in a role
- Share with group: What did ASK reveal?

**Application to Home Finance:**
- Account executive role: ASK breakdown
- Collections specialist: ASK breakdown
- Underwriter: ASK breakdown

**Discussion Questions:**
1. Most hiring mistakes: Which element was missing? (Usually Skill or Knowledge clarity)
2. How does ASK change how you interview?
3. What's one job in your team that needs clearer ASK definition?

**This Week's Action:**
- Design one role in your team using ASK framework
- Use it to assess: Is current person right fit?

---

### SESSION 10: Interview Techniques — How to Hire the Right Person

**Learning Objectives:**
- Learn behavioral interviewing
- Avoid hiring mistakes
- Conduct structured interviews

**Key Concept:** Ask for real examples. Probe deep. Notice how they handle feedback.

**Four Principles:**
1. Ask open-ended questions (real stories, not hypotheticals)
2. Listen for specifics (concrete examples = credible)
3. Ask follow-up questions (makes them think)
4. Notice response to feedback (how they handle it matters)

**Exercise:**
- Role play: Behavioral interview
- One person interviews, one person is candidate
- Practice: Listen, ask follow-ups, notice patterns

**Red Flags:**
- Blame shifting ("My manager didn't understand me")
- No accountability ("They messed it up, not me")
- Defensiveness when questioned

**Green Flags:**
- Takes responsibility ("I made a mistake, learned X")
- Coachable ("That's a good point, I hadn't thought of it")
- Self-aware ("I was micromanaging, realized it, changed")

**This Week's Action:**
- If hiring: Conduct one interview using behavior framework
- If not: Practice with peer—role play interviewing

---

### SESSION 11: Making the Hire Decision

**Learning Objectives:**
- Use objective decision matrix
- Say no to "good enough"
- Set up for success

**Key Concept:** Decide based on capability and fit, not likeability.

**Decision Matrix:**
- List 7 key criteria
- Rate each candidate on each
- Add up scores
- Highest wins (not your gut)

**Exercise:**
- If hiring: Use Appendix 8 (Hiring Decision Matrix)
- If not: Use matrix to assess current team
- Discuss: Are people in right roles?

**Onboarding Plan:**
- Use Appendix 9 (Onboarding Checklist)
- First 90 days critical
- Daily check-ins for first month
- Structured progression

**Discussion Questions:**
1. How do you usually make hiring decisions? (Gut vs. matrix?)
2. What would change if you used objective criteria?
3. Who on your current team might be in wrong role?

**This Week's Action:**
- If hiring: Make decision based on matrix (not gut)
- If not: Have conversation with someone potentially misaligned with role

## PART 5: KNOW YOUR PEOPLE (Weeks 12-13)

### SESSION 12: The Capability × Commitment Matrix

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand matrix framework
- Map your team
- See different management needs

**Key Concept:** Different people need different things. Don't manage everyone the same.

**Four Types:**
1. **A Players** (High capability + High commitment) → Give autonomy, stretch, recognition
2. **Mercenaries** (High capability + Low commitment) → Give valuable work, clear boundaries
3. **Learners** (Low capability + High commitment) → Give structure, coaching, development path
4. **Problem Employees** (Low capability + Low commitment) → Have conversation, options

**Exercise:**
- Use Appendix 3 (Capability × Commitment Matrix)
- Map your team
- For each person: What do they need from you?

**Real Scenario:**
- MS Dhoni and the Indian cricket team
- Different types required different management
- That's how he built world-class team

**Discussion Questions:**
1. Who's your A Player? How are you developing them?
2. Do you have Mercenaries? How are you managing them?
3. Who's a Learner? What support do they need?
4. Is anyone a Problem Employee? What's your plan?

**This Week's Action:**
- For each quadrant, do one specific action
- A Players: Give bigger stretch
- Learners: Provide structured coaching
- Mercenaries: Clarify boundaries and value
- Problems: Have honest conversation

---

### SESSION 13: Managing the Four Types

**Learning Objectives:**
- Know how to manage each type
- Have right conversations
- Move people toward A Player potential

**Key Concept:** A Players drive results. But Learners become A Players if you develop them.

**A Player Management:**
- Give autonomy
- Provide stretch opportunities
- Develop leadership capability
- Keep them engaged

**Mercenary Management:**
- Clear role and expectations
- Valuable (to them) work
- Professional relationship
- Plan for exit

**Learner Management:**
- Provide structure
- Coach regularly
- Celebrate progress
- Clear path to next level

**Problem Employee Management:**
- Have direct conversation
- Understand root cause
- Decide: Develop, move, or exit
- Execute respectfully

**Exercise:**
- Choose one person from your team
- What quadrant are they in?
- What do they need?
- What's your action plan?
- Share with group for feedback

**Discussion Questions:**
1. Who's your biggest success story developing someone?
2. When have you moved someone or exited someone? What was hard?
3. How will you know when a Learner becomes an A Player?

**This Week's Action:**
- Have one targeted conversation based on person's quadrant
- Execute planned action

## PART 6: DEVELOP PEOPLE (Weeks 14-18)

### SESSION 14: Stop Doing Everything

**Learning Objectives:**
- Identify work you shouldn't be doing
- Hand off strategically
- Develop team capability

**Key Concept:** Work you do is work your team doesn't learn.

**The Math:**
- You do it: 1 hour
- You teach it: 6 hours over 3 weeks
- But then: They do it, you're done

**Exercise:**
- List 3-5 tasks you do regularly
- For each: Who could learn to do this?
- For each: Plan the hand-off
  - Week 1: Do it together
  - Week 2: They lead, you support
  - Week 3: They do it, you review
  - Week 4: They own it

**Real Scenario:**
- Vikram (ops manager) handed off reporting
- Anita took it over
- In 4 months: Vikram's hours dropped from 55 to 45/week
- Anita got promoted 18 months later

**This Week's Action:**
- Hand off one task
- First session: Do it together

---

### SESSION 15: Allow Failure and Learning

**Learning Objectives:**
- Distinguish learning failure from negligence
- Create psychological safety
- Encourage experimentation

**Key Concept:** Failure is information. When managed right, it accelerates learning.

**Three Types of Failure:**
1. **Learning Failure** (tried something new, didn't work) → Encourage
2. **Process Failure** (didn't follow process) → Address
3. **Negligence** (didn't care) → Consequences

**Psychological Safety:**
- People try new things
- People admit mistakes early
- People ask for help
- People bring problems before they explode

**Exercise:**
- Tell story: A time you failed and learned from it
- Pair share: What did you learn?
- Group discuss: How do we create safety for this?

**Failure Debrief:**
- What did you try?
- What happened?
- What did you learn?
- What would you do differently?
- How will you adjust?

**This Week's Action:**
- When someone fails: Have learning conversation, not blame conversation
- Notice and celebrate learning

---

### SESSION 16: Reframing Feedback — From Punishment to Growth

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand feedback as gift
- See why managers avoid it
- Reframe for growth

**Key Concept:** Feedback isn't judgment. It's data to help people improve.

**Why Managers Avoid Feedback:**
- Uncomfortable (emotional)
- Unclear how to give it (skill gap)
- Fear of hurting feelings (misplaced kindness)
- Not knowing it matters (underestimate impact)

**Feedback as Gift:**
- I care about your growth
- I notice something that could help you
- Here's what I see
- Here's how you can improve
- Here's how I'll support you

**Exercise:**
- Feedback exercise: Give feedback to peer
- Peer gives feedback on how you gave it
- Practice receiving feedback without defensiveness

**Discussion Questions:**
1. What makes feedback scary for you?
2. How do you react when you receive feedback?
3. What feedback are you avoiding giving right now?

**This Week's Action:**
- Give one piece of feedback using the framework
- Notice how person responds

---

### SESSION 17: How to Give Feedback Effectively

**Learning Objectives:**
- Master three types of feedback
- Create feedback rhythm
- Make feedback normal

**Key Concept:** Daily positive + Weekly coaching + Monthly accountability = culture of feedback

**Three Types:**
1. **Positive Reinforcement** (quick, specific) → Daily
2. **Coaching on Gaps** (calm, collaborative) → Weekly
3. **Accountability** (direct, clear) → As needed

**The Feedback Conversation (8 steps):**
1. Set up well (private, intentional)
2. Describe behavior (specific, observable)
3. Explain impact (why it matters)
4. Get perspective (listen)
5. Partner on solution (collaborative)
6. Follow up (check progress)

**Use Appendix 11** (Feedback Conversation Worksheet)

**Exercise:**
- Use worksheet to plan feedback
- Practice conversation
- Get feedback on how you delivered it

**Feedback Rhythm:**
- Monday: Notice what you want to reinforce
- Wed: Give coaching feedback
- Friday: Check in on progress
- Weekly: Give 3 pieces of feedback

**This Week's Action:**
- Give one piece of feedback daily
- Use all three types during week

---

### SESSION 18: Difficult Conversations That Matter

**Learning Objectives:**
- Address three hard conversations
- Build courage
- Resolve issues early

**Key Concept:** Difficult conversations now prevent bigger problems later.

**Three Conversations:**
1. **Performance** ("You're not meeting standards")
2. **Promotion** ("You're not getting the role")
3. **Exit** ("We're parting ways")

**Setup:**
- Private
- Scheduled
- Prepare what you'll say
- Ready to listen

**Performance Conversation:**
- Be direct about issue
- Ask for perspective
- Give specific feedback
- Create improvement plan OR exit plan

**Promotion Conversation:**
- If not ready: "Here's what you need to develop. Here's how I'll help. You could be ready in 12-18 months."
- If not happening: "I want to be honest. This role won't open up for you. You might want to explore other companies."

**Exit Conversation:**
- Direct ("We're going to part ways")
- Not sentimental
- Respectful
- Clear logistics (severance, exit date, etc.)

**Exercise:**
- Role play: One person has conversation, one receives
- Practice being direct without being harsh
- Practice listening without defending

**Real Scenarios:**
- Performance conversation that worked
- Difficult exit handled well
- How early conversation prevented crisis

**This Week's Action:**
- Identify one difficult conversation you're avoiding
- Schedule it
- Have it
- Debrief with mentor

## PART 7: PERFORMANCE MANAGEMENT (Weeks 19-23)

### SESSION 19: Setting Clear Expectations

**Learning Objectives:**
- Make expectations crystal clear
- Connect to business goals
- Ensure understanding

**Key Concept:** Vague expectations lead to vague performance.

**Clear Expectation:**
- Specific outcome (not general)
- Measurable (not subjective)
- Timeline (not open-ended)
- Why it matters (connection to business)
- What good looks like (examples)

**Exercise:**
- Take one goal you set with team
- Rate: How clear is this? (1-5)
- Rewrite to make clearer
- Share rewrite with group

**Conversation:**
- "Here's what I need: [specific outcome]"
- "Here's why it matters: [business reason]"
- "Here's what success looks like: [specific examples]"
- "Do you understand? Any questions?"

**This Week's Action:**
- Have clarity conversation with each person
- Use conversation framework

---

### SESSION 20: Recognition

**Learning Objectives:**
- Make recognition systematic
- Notice what matters
- Celebrate wins

**Key Concept:** Recognition is how people feel their work matters.

**How to Recognize:**
- Specific (not generic)
- Timely (not delayed)
- Public (when appropriate)
- Connected to value

**What to Notice:**
- Results (hitting targets)
- Effort (going extra mile)
- Growth (learning something new)
- Values (showing company values)
- Help (helping teammates)

**Exercise:**
- Use Appendix 15 (Recognition Tracker)
- Track recognitions this week
- Bring tracker to next session
- Group review: Who's recognized? Who's missing?

**This Week's Action:**
- Recognize 3 people specifically
- Use format: "I noticed [behavior]. Here's why it mattered: [impact]."

---

### SESSION 21: Coaching and Feedback

**Learning Objectives:**
- Create ongoing feedback
- Coach to capability
- Track progress

**Key Concept:** Feedback isn't annual. It's ongoing.

**Coaching Approach:**
- 1-on-1s (weekly minimum)
- Observation (watch how they work)
- Feedback (ongoing)
- Support (make them successful)
- Stretch (give them growth opportunities)

**Tracking Progress:**
- Goal: 40 loans per month
- Month 1: 30 (on track for 45-day delay)
- Coaching: Here's what's working, here's what to adjust
- Month 2: 35 (improving)
- Coaching: Keep going, you're learning
- Month 3: 40 (hit target)

**Use Appendix 10** (1-on-1 Meeting Template)

**Exercise:**
- Plan 1-on-1 for this week using template
- Identify: What to listen for, what to coach on, what to celebrate

**This Week's Action:**
- Have 1-on-1 with each person
- Use coaching framework

---

### SESSION 22: The Appraisal Process

**Learning Objectives:**
- Conduct fair appraisals
- Connect to compensation
- Plan next year

**Key Concept:** Appraisal is summary of year, not surprise.

**What to Review:**
- Performance vs. goals (quantitative)
- Competencies (qualitative)
- Growth (skill development)
- Fit (culture alignment)

**Performance Levels:**
- Exceeds Expectations → Raise + bonus + development
- Meets Expectations → Fair compensation + development
- Below Expectations → Performance plan + support
- Not Acceptable → Formal action or exit

**Use Appendix 14** (Appraisal Form)

**Exercise:**
- Fill out sample appraisal
- Discuss: How do you handle conversations with people below expectations?

**Appraisal Conversation:**
1. Review goals and performance
2. Share assessment
3. Get their assessment
4. Discuss development
5. Discuss compensation
6. Plan for next year

**This Week's Action:**
- Schedule appraisals (if annual cycle)
- Begin having conversations

---

### SESSION 23: Consequences and Paths Forward

**Learning Objectives:**
- Make decisions based on performance
- Manage different outcomes
- Drive improvement

**Key Concept:** Consequences connect performance to outcomes. That's how you drive change.

**Decision Tree:**
- **Exceeds:** Recognize, promote, develop further
- **Meets:** Fair pay, continue development, prepare for advancement
- **Below:** Performance plan, support, escalation if no improvement
- **Not Acceptable:** Exit process

**Performance Improvement Plan:**
- Use Appendix 12
- Specific goals
- Timeline (usually 60-90 days)
- Support provided
- Consequences if not met

**Exit Process:**
- Use Appendix 17 (Exit Checklist)
- Knowledge transfer
- Respectful transition
- Learn what went wrong

**Exercise:**
- Scenario: Someone not meeting expectations
- What's your plan? Develop? Move? Exit?
- Role play: Having the conversation

**Reflection on 6-Month Journey:**
- What have you learned?
- How is your leadership different?
- How is your team different?
- What's next?

**This Week's Action:**
- Identify one person where a decision is needed (advance, develop, move, exit)
- Make decision
- Execute respectfully
