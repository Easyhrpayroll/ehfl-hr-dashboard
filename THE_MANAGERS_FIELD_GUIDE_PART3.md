## Chapter 7: Results + Relationships — The Culture That Works

### The Two Traps Every Manager Falls Into

**Trap 1: Results Only**

You focus entirely on hitting numbers. Team members are resources. Work is about output. Relationships don't matter—only the bottom line.

What happens:
- People work hard because they have to
- Turnover is high
- People leave as soon as they find something better
- Innovation drops (no one takes risks)
- When times get hard, people bail
- The top talent leaves first (they have options)

You hit short-term numbers. You miss long-term growth.

---

**Trap 2: Relationships Only**

You focus on being liked. Team members are friends. Work is about getting along. Results don't matter—only that everyone is comfortable.

What happens:
- People like you, but they don't respect you
- Standards drop
- Mediocre performance is tolerated
- No one gets pushed to grow
- The business underperforms
- You eventually get fired (or demoted) because results aren't there

People are happy while you're failing.

---

### The Concept: Results + Relationships

The best-performing teams have both.

This isn't a compromise. It's not "balance between." It's integration.

**Results** = Outcomes matter. Goals matter. Performance matters. Quality matters. You hold people accountable to standards.

**Relationships** = People matter. Their growth matters. Their wellbeing matters. Trust matters. You invest in people.

When you have both, something magical happens:

People work hard because they want to (not just because they have to). They bring ideas because they feel valued. They stay during hard times because they're invested. They're willing to be stretched because they trust you're invested in their growth.

This creates a culture where people perform at their best *and* feel valued.

### The Five-Step Framework: Building Results + Relationships Culture

#### Step 1: Clarity on Outcomes

People need to know what success looks like. Not vague. Specific.

Not: "We need to grow."

Yes: "We need to increase loan approvals by 15% this quarter. That means moving from 150/month to 175/month. Here's why: [business reason]. Here's what success looks like for each function."

**How to do it:**
- Translate business targets into team targets
- Translate team targets into individual targets
- Make sure people understand the *why*
- Make sure they understand what *good* looks like

**Common mistake:** Assuming people understand. They don't. Say it, repeat it, make sure they get it.

---

#### Step 2: Authority to Act

People need the autonomy to pursue outcomes. Not full freedom. But real decision-making authority.

**Results-only managers** say: "Hit this number. Report to me daily. I'll decide strategy."

**Relationships-only managers** say: "Do whatever makes you happy. No pressure. We're here for you."

**Results + Relationships managers** say: "Here's the outcome we need. Here's the constraint [budget, timeline, quality]. Within that, you decide how. You have authority to try, to fail, to adjust. Report weekly on progress and learning."

**How to do it:**
- Define the outcome
- Define the constraint (budget, timeline, scope)
- Define the decision authority (What can they decide? What do they escalate?)
- Get out of the way
- Coach, don't direct

**Common mistake:** Giving authority without clear boundaries. That's actually just abdication. Clear constraints + clear authority = autonomy people can use well.

---

#### Step 3: Real Support and Coaching

People need to know you're invested in their success. Not just in their output. In their growth and development.

This means:
- You check in on how they're doing (not just results)
- You coach them through difficulty (not just criticize)
- You celebrate their growth (not just their wins)
- You help them see their capability (not just their mistakes)

**How to do it:**
- Have regular one-on-ones (weekly, at least for new people)
- Ask about obstacles: "What's getting in your way? How can I help?"
- Coach to capability: "Here's how I would approach this. Here's what you're thinking. Let's think through it together."
- Notice effort and growth: "I see you trying this new approach. That took courage."

**Common mistake:** Thinking support means doing it for them. It doesn't. Support means coaching them to be better.

---

#### Step 4: Accountability That Builds Character

Accountability isn't punishment. It's clarity.

Accountability is: We agreed on this. This is where we are. This is what needs to happen. Let's do it.

When someone isn't hitting targets, accountability looks like:

1. **Understand:** "Help me understand what's happening. What obstacles are you hitting?"
2. **Support:** "Here's what I see we can do. What do you think?"
3. **Agreement:** "Okay, here's what we're both going to do."
4. **Follow-up:** "How's it going? What are you learning?"
5. **Consequence:** If progress isn't happening: "We're not where we need to be. Here's what that means..."

This builds character because it teaches: *Standards matter. Growth happens through pushing through difficulty.*

**Common mistake:** Either avoiding accountability ("You'll get it eventually") or harsh accountability ("Fix it or you're out"). Both break the Results + Relationships dynamic. The first says results don't matter. The second says relationships don't matter.

---

#### Step 5: Recognition and Growth

People need to feel their contribution matters. Not just in a salary. In actual recognition.

**Results-only recognition:** "You hit your number. Good."

**Relationships-only recognition:** "You're a great person. Keep being you."

**Results + Relationships recognition:** "You hit 180 loans this month, which was 5% over target. I noticed you got customers we thought were impossible—here's how that matters to the business. I also noticed you mentored Priya on objection handling. That's leadership. I wanted you to know both things matter."

**How to do it:**
- Notice results
- Notice effort and learning
- Notice character and values
- Say it specifically
- Make people feel their work has meaning

**Common mistake:** Only recognizing big wins. The real culture is built in small moments. Noticing someone stay late to help a customer. Noticing someone admit they were wrong. Noticing someone take on a stretch project. These moments matter.

### What Results + Relationships Culture Looks Like in Action

**Scenario:**

A team member, Ravi, isn't hitting his targets. He's at 70% of his monthly goal with 10 days left.

**Results-Only Manager Response:**
"Ravi, you're at 70%. You need to be at 100% by month-end. I expect you to work weekends if needed. You have one week to catch up or we need to have a different conversation. I'll check in daily."

Result: Ravi works hard. Maybe hits 85%. Feels used. Updates resume.

---

**Relationships-Only Manager Response:**
"Ravi, I see you're at 70%. That's okay. You're doing your best. Maybe targets are too high for you. Or maybe you need a less pressured role. Let's just focus on having fun and being sustainable."

Result: Ravi feels no pressure. Stays at 70%. Company misses numbers. Ravi never grows because there's no push.

---

**Results + Relationships Manager Response:**
"Ravi, I see you're at 70%. We need to hit 100%. Before we figure out how, I want to understand what's happening. What's getting in your way?"

Ravi: "I've lost two big leads this month. They chose a competitor."

Manager: "Okay. Help me understand what happened with those two. What was the gap?"

Ravi: "I think I'm not as good at handling price objections. The competitor has a lower rate."

Manager: "That makes sense. Are you interested in getting better at this?"

Ravi: "Yes. I want to hit target."

Manager: "Okay. Here's what I'm thinking: Let's listen to the recordings of those two calls together. I'll show you how I would have handled the objection. Then I want you to take the next three lead calls and try the approach. I'll listen in. We'll debrief after each one. I think you'll get better quickly."

Ravi: "Okay. I can do that."

Manager: "And I want you to know: I believe you can hit 100% this month and stay there. This is learnable. You've hit target before. You'll do it again. Let's do this together."

Result: Ravi feels supported. He's invested in learning. He hits target. He's also better at objection handling going forward. He feels valued and pushed at the same time.

### Why This Matters in Indian Home Finance

In the Indian NBFC space, both traps are common.

Some managers go full results-only: "We're in a competitive business. Relationships are a luxury. Hit your number or leave."

Result: Massive turnover. Burnt-out teams. People gaming the system (selling products that don't fit customers to hit targets).

Other managers go full relationships-only: "We're a family. We take care of each other. Numbers will follow."

Result: Mediocre performance. No accountability. When the business hits a downturn, there's no cushion, and everyone panics.

The winning approach: "We're a high-performing team that cares about each other. We hit ambitious targets. We also invest in each other's growth. We work hard and we do it sustainably."

### What Good Managers Do

Good managers show up at every level of the five-step framework.

**On outcomes:** They're crystal clear. They repeat. They make sure people understand.

**On autonomy:** They set boundaries and give decision-making authority. They resist the urge to control.

**On support:** They're available. They coach. They help people see their own capability.

**On accountability:** They address gaps. They do it constructively. They focus on growth, not punishment.

**On recognition:** They notice. They celebrate. They make people feel their work matters.

Good managers also model the culture themselves. They work hard (results) but they also invest in relationships. People see that both matter.

### What Bad Managers Do

**Bad Results-Only Manager:**
- Sets targets but no real support
- Doesn't know their team members
- Only talks to people when there's a problem
- Celebrates results, doesn't celebrate growth
- People feel used
- Turnover is high
- When a hard quarter hits, people bail

**Bad Relationships-Only Manager:**
- Avoids hard conversations
- Doesn't hold people to standards
- Tries to be everyone's friend
- Celebrates people but not performance
- People don't respect the role
- Standards drop
- Business underperforms

**Bad Managers with Both Failures:**
- Inconsistent: Sometimes results-focused, sometimes relationships-focused
- Don't follow up: Set targets but don't coach or check in
- Unclear on what matters: People don't know if they should prioritize results or relationships
- Create anxiety: People don't know what to expect

### Real Scenario: The Manager Who Transformed Her Culture

Isha was a lending manager at an NBFC. When she started, her team was burnt out. High turnover. People were hitting targets but they were exhausted. New people would come in, work hard for 3-4 months, then quit.

Isha decided to change. She decided to build Results + Relationships culture intentionally.

**Month 1:** She worked on clarity. She didn't just announce targets. She explained them. "Here's our goal: 500 loans this quarter. Here's why: We're expanding to a new market. Success there depends on establishing a presence here. You're not just hitting a number—you're building the foundation for growth."

**Month 2:** She worked on autonomy. She stopped approving every decision. She said: "You know your customers better than I do. If you think a rate adjustment will get a deal done and it's within this range, go ahead. You decide. Tell me weekly what you learned."

**Month 3:** She worked on support. She had real one-on-ones. Not just: "Are you hitting target?" But: "What's frustrating you? What would make your job better? What do you want to learn?"

**Month 4:** She worked on accountability. One person wasn't improving. Instead of threatening, she coached: "You're stuck at 30 loans/month. You're capable of 40. Let me help. Here's what I'm seeing... Let's work on this together."

**Month 5:** She worked on recognition. She started a weekly call-out: "This week, Rohit handled a customer complaint by really listening instead of defending. That's how we build loyalty. Priya closed three deals that she thought weren't possible—she didn't give up, she found a creative solution. That's mindset we need."

**Result:** Turnover dropped dramatically. People were still hitting targets, but they were less burnt out. And they were staying because they felt valued *and* pushed.

### Conversation Script: The Results + Relationships Conversation with Your Team

This is worth having with your team explicitly:

---

**You:** "I want to talk about how we work together as a team. We have ambitious goals. We need to hit them. And we also need to take care of each other. Both matter equally."

**Team:** [Listening]

**You:** "That means: You'll hear me push you to be better. I'll also invest in your growth. You'll hit targets. I'll also celebrate how you got there. You have freedom to make decisions. I'll also have your back. Does that make sense?"

**Team:** "Yes."

**You:** "Good. So if I'm only pushing and not supporting, call me on it. If I'm only being supportive and not pushing, that's also a problem. We need both. Okay?"

**Team:** "Okay."

### Reflection Questions

1. **What's your natural default—results-focused or relationships-focused?** What's the danger of staying there?

2. **Can you think of a time someone pushed you hard AND made you feel valued?** How did you respond?

3. **Who on your team feels unsupported? What would it take to support them better?**

4. **Who on your team isn't being held to standards? What would accountability look like?**

One action. One week. Build the Results + Relationships culture step by step.

## Summary: Part 3 — Building Culture

Your job as a manager isn't just to hit targets. It's to build a culture where people perform at their best while also feeling valued and growing.

This requires both Results and Relationships. Not one or the other. Both.

Results without Relationships leads to burnout, turnover, and people gaming the system.

Relationships without Results leads to mediocrity and eventually failure.

Results + Relationships creates a culture where people are engaged, growing, and performing at their best.

The five-step framework (Outcomes, Autonomy, Support, Accountability, Recognition) is how you build that culture.
