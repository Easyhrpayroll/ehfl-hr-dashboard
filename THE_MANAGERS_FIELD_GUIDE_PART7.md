## Chapter 19-23: Performance Management Framework (Condensed)

### The Arc of Performance Management

Performance management isn't just reviews. It's an ongoing system:

**1. Clarity (Chapter 19):** Set expectations
**2. Recognition (Chapter 20):** Celebrate wins
**3. Feedback (Chapter 21):** Continuous coaching
**4. Appraisal (Chapter 22):** Formal assessment
**5. Decisions (Chapter 23):** Consequences and paths forward

### Chapter 19: Setting Clear Expectations

Before anything else, people need to know what success looks like.

**What to Do:**
- Set specific outcomes (not vague goals)
- Explain why those outcomes matter
- Define what good performance looks like
- Make it measurable where possible
- Align with business goals

**Example:**
- Bad: "We need to increase sales."
- Good: "Your target is 40 loans per month with 90%+ customer satisfaction. This drives revenue and customer lifetime value. Here's how it fits into our quarterly goal."

**Frequency:** Set at the start of the year. Review quarterly. Adjust as needed.

### Chapter 20: Recognition

Recognition is how you make people feel their work matters.

**What to Do:**
- Catch people doing things right
- Be specific (don't just say "good job")
- Do it frequently
- Public and private recognition matter
- Connect to company values and impact

**Example:**
"Priya, I saw how you handled that customer complaint. Instead of defending our process, you listened to their concern first. You turned a frustrated customer into a loyal one. That's exactly the customer-focused behavior we're trying to build."

**Frequency:** Daily or at least weekly.

### Chapter 21: Coaching and Feedback

Ongoing feedback that addresses gaps and reinforces strengths.

**What to Do:**
- Give feedback frequently (not just at review time)
- Use the frameworks from Part 6
- Balance positive and constructive
- Make it actionable
- Follow up to see if behavior changed

### Chapter 22: The Appraisal Process

Formal assessment of performance, typically annual or twice-yearly.

**What to Do:**
- Review actual performance against expectations
- Involve the person (their self-assessment matters)
- Be honest about performance level
- Connect to compensation decisions
- Identify development areas

**Performance Levels:**
- **Exceeds Expectations:** Consistently performs above standard
- **Meets Expectations:** Performs at expected level
- **Needs Improvement:** Below expected level (requires plan)
- **Not Acceptable:** Significant gap (requires formal action)

### Chapter 23: Consequences and Paths Forward

Based on performance, different paths:

**Exceeds Expectations:**
- Recognize with bonus, raise, or promotion
- Give more responsibility
- Develop for next level

**Meets Expectations:**
- Fair compensation
- Continued development
- Prepare for advancement

**Needs Improvement:**
- Clear performance improvement plan
- Specific goals and timeline
- Regular check-ins
- Support or consequences

**Not Acceptable:**
- Formal notice
- Clear expectations to change
- Timeline for improvement
- Exit strategy if not met

### The Performance Management Conversation Framework

**1-on-1 during the year (Continuous):**
- "How's progress on your goals?"
- "What's going well? What's hard?"
- "Here's what I'm seeing you do well... Here's where I want to help you improve..."

**Formal review conversation (Annual):**
- "Let's review your performance against your goals."
- "Here's my assessment... What's your assessment?"
- "Here's what I want to see next year..."
- "Here's how I'll support your development..."

### Real Scenario: The Manager Who Got Performance Right

Arun managed a team of eight. He took performance management seriously.

**During the Year:**
- Monthly 1-on-1s where they reviewed goals and gave feedback
- Quick recognitions when he saw good behavior
- Coaching on areas of development

**Annual Review:**
- Arun and each person came to the conversation with assessments
- They discussed together
- Arun was honest about performance level
- They planned for the next year

Result:
- His team knew where they stood all year (no surprises)
- Good performance was recognized
- Problems were addressed early
- People felt developed, not just managed

Turnover was low. Performance was high. People wanted to work for Arun.

### Quick Reference Framework

**Performance Management Checklist:**

- [ ] Are expectations clear?
- [ ] Do I recognize good performance?
- [ ] Do I give frequent feedback?
- [ ] Do I address gaps promptly?
- [ ] Do I have formal reviews?
- [ ] Do I connect performance to consequences?
- [ ] Do I develop people based on their level?

## Summary: Part 7 — Performance Management

Performance management is the system that makes everything else stick.

Without it: Good performers coast. Poor performers stay poor. Nothing changes.

With it: Everyone knows where they stand. Good performance is recognized. Poor performance is addressed. People develop.

The system is:
- **Clarity** on expectations
- **Recognition** of wins
- **Coaching** on gaps
- **Formal review** of progress
- **Consequences** based on performance

Get this right, and your team will perform at their best.

### The 30-Day Transformation Plan

If you start implementing what you've learned in this book today, here's what the first 30 days look like:

**Week 1: Assess and Understand**
- Do the self-assessment (Chapter 1)
- Map your team on the Capability × Commitment matrix (Chapter 12)
- Understand where you are

**Week 2: Start Developing**
- Have a development conversation with your A Player
- Start a coaching conversation with one Learner
- Give three pieces of recognition

**Week 3: Set Expectations**
- Clarify outcomes with your team
- Explain the Results + Relationships culture
- Have the feedback conversation

**Week 4: Build Systems**
- Establish regular 1-on-1s
- Create a feedback rhythm
- Plan recognition for good performance

**By Day 30:**
- Your team knows what you expect
- People feel valued
- You've started developing capability
- You're not doing everything yourself

This isn't the complete transformation. But it's the start.

## The Manager's Operating System

Here's your weekly routine to lead effectively:

**Monday Morning:** Plan the week
- What's the team focused on?
- Who needs attention?
- What feedback do I need to give?

**Tuesday-Thursday:** Execute
- Hold 1-on-1s
- Observe behavior
- Give feedback
- Coach on development

**Friday:** Reflect
- What did I see this week?
- Who grew? Who struggled?
- What's next?

**Ongoing:**
- Monthly check-in with each person
- Quarterly review of goals
- Annual formal appraisal

## Conclusion: The Manager You're Becoming

If you implement what's in this book, here's what changes:

**You Stop Being a Technician:**
- You stop doing the work yourself
- You develop people to do it

**You Become a Leader:**
- You create clarity and direction
- You build trust and psychological safety
- You develop people to be better than you

**Your Team:**
- Performs better (because they're capable and motivated)
- Stays longer (because they feel developed and valued)
- Takes more initiative (because you've given them authority)
- Becomes less dependent on you (because they're capable)

**Your Job:**
- Becomes sustainable (you're not doing everything)
- Becomes meaningful (you're developing people)
- Becomes effective (your team delivers results)

**Your Career:**
- Becomes promotable (you've built a team that doesn't need you)
- Becomes impactful (you're multiplying through people)
- Becomes fulfilling (you're making a difference)

This is the transition from individual contributor to leader.

It's not easy. It requires uncomfortable conversations. It requires letting go. It requires trusting people.

But it's the work that matters most.

And it's what makes you a manager people want to work for.
